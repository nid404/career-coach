### When I go blank reading under pressure, suspect state, not capacity
- Origin: SBI Life week 2 — went blank on PL's document under time pressure. Believed blankness meant inadequacy; reality: anxiety was eating the working memory needed to build understanding. Evidence it's state not trait: passed CA, generated sharp lessons the same day.
- Application: comprehension drops under stakes → slow down, build the model deliberately rather than waiting for instant insight.

### On a new or ambiguous ask, lead with a working answer, not a blank question
- Origin: SBI Life week 2 — defaulted to "what do you want?" rather than forming a read. Reality: "here's my read, where am I off?" gathers the same context, kills the blankness, and reads senior.
- Application: any ambiguous task or question from PL / senior → one sentence hypothesis first, then verify. (Connects to: How to Ask Questions — show your work.)

### Own errors by leading with the correction and reasoning, not concealment or apology
- Origin: two slide errors this week. Instinct was to minimize; reality: character reads come from patterns across many data points, not single cells. Leading with the fix and the reasoning turns an error into a decision point.
- Application: any discovered mistake before or after a PL-facing handoff.

### Match the register: social questions want engagement, content questions want a hypothesis plus verification
- Origin: week 2 social dynamics with PL. Reality: bluffing a social answer erodes reliability; an honest "I'll check" on a content question is stronger than a confident wrong answer. Believability is built on reliable words.
- Application: PL asks something personal or opinion-based → engage (humor fine); PL asks something factual → hypothesis + "let me verify" if uncertain.
