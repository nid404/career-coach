# Peak: The New Science of Expertise
https://commoncog.com/peak-book-summary/

## Overview

This article summarizes K. Anders Ericsson's *Peak*, a 2016 book examining three decades of expertise research. Ericsson wrote it partly to clarify misconceptions from Malcolm Gladwell's popular "10,000-hour rule," which oversimplified his actual findings.

## Types of Practice

**Naive Practice**: Mere repetition without improvement strategy. As one instructor notes, "Practice doesn't make perfect. Practice makes permanent"—repeating poor technique only entrenches bad habits.

**Purposeful Practice**: More effective than naive practice, featuring four key properties:
- Complete mental focus during sessions
- Discomfort that pushes beyond your comfort zone
- Immediate feedback with technical adjustments
- Specific, well-defined goals rather than vague aims

## Mental Representations and Deliberate Practice

Ericsson argues expertise depends on quality mental representations—organized knowledge patterns in long-term memory enabling rapid, effective responses. Expert violinists, chess players, and surgeons succeed through superior mental models built over time.

**Deliberate Practice** requires:
- Fields with established training methods (music, chess, mathematics)
- Coaching guidance, especially initially
- Progressive mental representation development
- Focused skill-building through targeted practice

## Practical Examples

The author highlights real-world applications:
- Chess players analyze grandmaster positions rather than play full games
- Benjamin Franklin improved writing by summarizing articles then recreating them
- Swimmer Natalie Coughlin dramatically improved by maintaining mental focus during practice
- Short, intense practice sessions proved more effective than lengthy, unfocused ones

## Critical Assessment

While *Peak* introduces deliberate practice accessibly, the reviewer notes limitations. Ericsson sidesteps emerging research on genetics' role in expertise and doesn't address practical implementation challenges. The book works best as an introductory text rather than a comprehensive practitioner's guide.
