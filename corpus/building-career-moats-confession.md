# Building Career Moats: A Confession
https://commoncog.com/building-career-moats-a-confession/

By Cedric Chin

## Overview

This article opens a series exploring Ray Dalio's *Principles* through the lens of career development. The author shares his personal journey of identifying weaknesses and building competitive advantages despite recognizing himself as "an average software engineer" lacking raw intelligence.

## Key Story: Strategic Positioning

The author recounts returning to Singapore after a Silicon Valley internship and honestly assessing his abilities. Rather than competing directly with peers on algorithmic skills, he identified an overlooked opportunity: Southeast Asia's importance to tech expansion would create demand for people skilled at establishing operations across diverse regional markets.

He leveraged three advantages:
- Access to rare insight about SEA's market potential
- Willingness to relocate when peers wouldn't
- Commitment to developing difficult-to-acquire skills through three years in Vietnam

## Central Framework: Three Questions

Drawing from Dalio's approach, the author emphasizes radical honesty:

1. "What do you know is true?"
2. "What are your goals?"
3. "What will you do to achieve them given reality?"

The author applies psychological pain as a signal—when you're avoiding discomfort, you're likely denying truth that matters for decision-making.

## Credibility & Intent

The author acknowledges he's created only one career moat so far, not yet meeting Dalio's standard of "believability" (three demonstrated successes plus articulated methodology). However, he commits to documenting his decision-making processes transparently, hoping his specific struggles illuminate principles applicable to readers' different circumstances.
