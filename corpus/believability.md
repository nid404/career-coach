# Believability
https://commoncog.com/believability/

## Origin

This concept originates from Ray Dalio's *Principles*, summarized in his essay "On Lying To Yourself."

## Purpose

The believability heuristic serves as an efficient tool for determining whose feedback to seek and whose opinions to weight during decision-making processes.

## Core Definition

"Believable people are people who have 1) a record of at least three relevant successes and 2) have great explanations of their approach when probed."

## Application Guidelines

When evaluating others' input:

1. **With more believable people:** Prioritize asking questions over debating. This approach proves more effective for discovering truth than argumentative exchanges.

2. **With peers:** Only engage in debate with those having comparable believability to yourself.

3. **With less believable people:** Invest minimal effort to identify any overlooked objections, but otherwise limit engagement time.

## Context

Dalio implemented this technique while leading Bridgewater Associates, one of the world's most successful hedge funds. He demonstrated its applicability beyond professional settings, including healthcare decision-making.

## Important Consideration

The technique requires accurate assessment of someone's believability within their specific domain. A venture capitalist's credibility on startup investments differs from their credibility on operational company management.

## Key Caution

Avoid reflexively dismissing lower-believability individuals. Instead, briefly examine their perspectives to ensure you're not overlooking fundamentally sound advice.
