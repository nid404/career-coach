# How to Read Self Help
https://commoncog.com/how-to-read-self-help/

By Cedric Chin

## The Problem with Self-Help

Self-help literature often faces justified criticism. While figures like Tony Robbins profit from selling success formulas and Rhonda Byrne's *The Secret* promotes New Thought ideology (which emphasizes mindset over action), much self-help lacks scientific rigor. As one psychology professor noted, self-help represents "a black hole of empirical research" where authors often avoid testing their claims to protect sales.

However, dismissing all self-help as worthless overlooks valuable practical knowledge. The key is learning how to read and evaluate it critically.

## Think of Self-Help as Technê

The author recommends treating self-help through the lens of *technê*, the ancient Greek concept of craft or practice—tacit knowledge that cannot be learned through reading alone but must be tested and refined through experience.

This reframing has important implications:

**Test Through Application.** Just as you wouldn't learn martial arts from books alone, self-help techniques require real-world testing. Discard approaches that don't produce lasting results or that you can't easily apply.

**Apply the Power Test.** Complex problems require powerful solutions. Be skeptical of mindset tweaks offered as universal solutions—"mindset changes only matter when combined with actual power to change your situation."

**Prioritize Believable Sources.** Learn from accomplished practitioners, not self-proclaimed gurus. Ray Dalio carries more weight than Tony Robbins because his track record demonstrates expertise.

**Backtest Ideas.** You don't need to test every technique. Examine whether new advice would have improved your past experiences. If a technique fails even one historical example, downgrade its usefulness.

## Why Read Self-Help at All?

Since experience teaches valuable lessons, why consume second-hand advice? The author argues that life is finite—we cannot run unlimited experiments. Learning from others' experiences accelerates development. Self-help represents merely one way of taking advice from people you'll never meet, making it worthwhile when approached strategically and skeptically.
