# How to Ask Questions (So Your Boss Doesn't Hate You)
https://commoncog.com/how-to-ask-questions/

By Cedric Chin

## The Problem with Poor Question-Asking

The author illustrates a cautionary tale: a new employee was given minimal training, then criticized for both asking questions and not asking for help—a no-win scenario. While the boss bore some responsibility, the underlying issue may have been ineffective question-asking throughout the year.

## A Learnable Skill

"Question asking is a skill — one you can get better at." The author observed early in his tech career that certain colleagues excelled at getting helpful responses while others struggled. Rather than viewing this as inevitable friction in new roles, he came to recognize question-asking as a developable capability.

## The Three-Step Structure

### 1. Pick a Good Time
Respect others' schedules by either catching them during natural breaks or explicitly requesting their time with duration estimates. For example: "Could I ask you about the shopping cart enhancements? It'll take about 15 minutes."

### 2. Show Your Work (The Critical Step)
This is where most people fail. Rather than asking "I can't solve A for task B. Help me pretty please?" demonstrate initiative by explaining attempted approaches and reasoning. Present it as: "I've tried approaches X and Y without success because of factors P and Q. Should I try approach Z, or do you have other advice?"

This demonstrates you're not seeking hand-holding but genuinely stuck after genuine effort.

### 3. Ask a Well-Formulated Question
Provide sufficient context using clear communication principles, ensuring your explanation is concise and comprehensible.

## Why This Matters

Senior employees fear becoming mentors to "time-wasting bloodsuckers" who continuously demand spoon-fed answers. Showing your work reassures them that you possess initiative and won't become a recurring drain on their time. As the author's former boss explained: "people like helping people who can be helped. So _be someone that can be helped!_"

This approach extends to building mentorship relationships, where demonstrating that you've acted on previous advice proves the mentor's time yields results.
