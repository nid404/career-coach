# Is the FIRE Movement a Good Idea?
https://commoncog.com/is-the-fire-movement-a-good-idea/

By Cedric Chin

## Introduction

The author explores the FIRE movement (Financial Independence, Retire Early) as a potential career strategy amid economic uncertainty. He contextualizes this discussion within broader concerns about job market bifurcation, automation, and technological disruption.

## The Career Uncertainty Context

The author's worldview is shaped by witnessing his father's job loss during the 1997 Asian financial crisis. This personal experience drives his philosophy that individuals should develop rare, valuable skills—what he calls "career moats"—to protect against future job market disruptions caused by globalization, automation, and artificial intelligence.

## Understanding FIRE

The FIRE movement combines two millennial anxieties: disillusionment with capitalism and the belief that investment returns exceed wage growth. Followers typically reduce wasteful expenses and maximize savings rates, often targeting 70 percent savings rates. The movement appeals particularly to tech workers who calculate compound interest and investment returns meticulously.

## The Author's Concerns

The author worries that FIRE relies on sustained market performance. His worst-case scenario imagines early retirees facing social upheaval from widespread automation-driven job losses. Without marketable skills, they couldn't re-enter a transformed labor market, despite substantial investment portfolios.

## Geographic Limitations

FIRE's frugality-focused resilience assumes access to affordable living alternatives. However, residents of city-states like Singapore lack rural options for cost reduction, making the strategy less viable than in countries with extensive, affordable hinterlands.

## Conclusion

While skeptical of full FIRE adoption, the author endorses its financial prudence principles—frugal living, early investment, and financial responsibility—as essential foundations for building career moats and long-term security.
