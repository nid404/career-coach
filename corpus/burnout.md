# Burnout: Symptoms, Causes, and Prevention
https://commoncog.com/g/burnout/

## What Is Burnout?

Burnout is a psychological state caused by prolonged work stress, characterized by three key dimensions: overwhelming exhaustion, feelings of cynicism, and a sense of ineffectiveness. The modern understanding frames burnout as "a stress experience within a social context" rather than purely an individual problem.

The **Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI)** is the widely-recognized measurement tool for assessing these three dimensions. Importantly, experiencing exhaustion alone doesn't mean someone is burnt out—all three dimensions must be present. Someone might feel exhausted from workplace conflict yet remain effective and engaged if their job feels meaningful.

## Developmental Models of Burnout

**The Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) Model** proposes that burnout develops when job demands (workload, stress, challenging interactions) exceed available resources (autonomy, feedback, support). Research shows employees with high job resources can handle greater demands without burning out.

**The Conservation of Resources (COR) Model** suggests burnout occurs when people lose or fail to gain valued resources—whether objects, conditions, personal characteristics, or time/energy. Notably, losses weigh more heavily than gains in this framework.

**The Areas of Worklife Scale (AWS)** identifies six critical domains:

1. **Workload** — manageable with recovery opportunities
2. **Control** — autonomy and decision-making capacity
3. **Reward** — monetary, social, and intrinsic recognition
4. **Community** — quality workplace relationships and support
5. **Fairness** — equitable processes and respect
6. **Values** — alignment between personal and organizational values

The greater the mismatch in these areas, the higher the burnout risk.

## Prevention Strategies

**Prevent exhaustion** through moderate workload, adequate sleep, exercise, and nutrition.

**Prevent cynicism** by improving workplace civility and strengthening collegial support.

**Prevent ineffectiveness** through recognition systems, regular feedback, and fair performance evaluations.

Critically, prevention requires **control**—the foundational element allowing employees to implement these strategies. Marissa Mayer's approach of identifying one non-negotiable personal activity (family dinner, exercise) and Cal Newport's seasonal work intensity cycles both leverage workplace autonomy.

## Recovery From Burnout

The research is sobering: "individual interventions don't work." Mindfulness, meditation, and similar approaches fail while people remain in the burnout-causing environment. The only proven path to recovery is removing yourself from that workplace and taking time off. However, recovery is guaranteed once distance is established—burnout diminishes naturally over time.

## Key Takeaway

Burnout is fundamentally an organizational problem, not merely an individual weakness. The most effective prevention comes through selecting workplaces offering adequate resources alongside demands, and through organizational leaders restructuring work environments using evidence-based models like the AWS.
