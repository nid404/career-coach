# Optimise for Usefulness
https://commoncog.com/optimise-for-usefulness/

By Cedric Chin

## Introduction

The article explores a central philosophy: adopt beliefs and practices that advance your goals, discarding those that don't. Citing Paul Graham's insight that "if you're trying to choose between two theories and one gives you an excuse for being lazy, the other one is probably right," the author argues that successful people instinctively optimize for practical usefulness rather than abstract truth.

## Modifying Mindsets for Usefulness

The author identifies "mindset tweaks"—worldview adjustments that enhance performance without changing knowledge or technique. A runner managing mental fatigue through self-talk exemplifies this: physical abilities remain unchanged, yet psychological reframing improves results.

Similarly, reframing work as "solving problems for people" rather than personal artistic expression reduces defensiveness about criticism. This shift enables better learning and persistence in fields like design and journalism.

## Discarding Unhelpful Mindsets

When a mindset actively hinders goal achievement, replacing it becomes essential. The author describes struggling at his previous company until adopting the perspective of treating difficulties as mastery-building opportunities. This reframing—similar to Cal Newport's "craftsman mindset"—proved transformative without changing external circumstances.

## Optimising Heuristics

Useful heuristics ("rules of thumb") improve life effectiveness. The author describes developing tests for identifying dismissively stubborn individuals and recognizing that debugging ability predicts programming competence. Ray Dalio's suggestion to formalize principles enables more efficient decision-making and easier principle refinement.

## Distilling Lessons from Experience

When problems arise, the author recommends ascending a usefulness ladder rather than settling for initial interpretations. Disappointment about a colleague's performance is less useful than developing generalizable frameworks for identifying personality types and effective management approaches applicable throughout one's career.

## Distilling Wrong Lessons

The article warns against jumping to limited conclusions. When a talented Taiwanese engineer departed after assignment to low-value projects, one manager concluded the market was too competitive for retaining quality talent. The author believed better retention strategies could succeed—a more useful interpretation that didn't prematurely close hiring avenues.

## Central Principle

"Optimising for usefulness" comprises four elements: recognizing mindset adjustments enhance effectiveness; actively replacing hindering perspectives; prioritizing high-usefulness lessons from experience; and avoiding premature, limiting conclusions. The blog applies this framework by selecting topics benefiting career development and encouraging readers to test ideas against their own goals rather than accepting claims at face value.
